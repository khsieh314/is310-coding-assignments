Digitized Cultural Object

The Odyssey translated by Emily Wilson in Digital Spaces

The Odyssey translated by Emily Wilson has become somewhat of a cultural phenomenon recently. Not only is it the first edition of that text translated by a woman, it's also the translation used by director Christopher Nolan in his summer 2026 blockbuster. The release and hype around this novel has brought back a renewed interest in classics; however this time, with online book store and libraries, it becomes a digital objects that is accessed by many.

Fun Fact: This image used on the cover was actually a recreation of an ancient Mosaic found in Crete as part of early Minoan culture. It implies the existence of a women-led cult.
But if you look closely, the slightly browner/off-colored parts are the really parts of the mosaic while the rest is a recreation.

Title: The Odyssey
Author: Homer
Translator: Emily Wilson
Publisher: W. W. Norton & Company
ISBN: 9780393089059

If you think about it, The Odyssey has gone on a journey of its own.
Originating as a form of oral tradition, it was initial spread by Homer (or a group of poets via Homeric question)
Transcribed by monks and other writers, then printing presses, and then modern day paper and hardback books
The new phase of it's journey has been turned into a digital object, online fourms like Goodreads provide a place for the object to be talked about
Amazon and libraries provide a place for the digitized object to be borrowed and sold online
Libraries offering digital access is the fully digitized form of this object that has held cultural significance for thousands of years
